## Why replication exists

A pod stores your records and their event history. Some teams want two copies of that history: a primary pod they use
every day and a replica (a backup, a failover candidate or a copy in another region).

Replication uses the same event log as the rest of the pod. Changes become completed events, which are sent to the
other pod over HTTPS and materialized there, so the events stay the source of truth on both sides.

## What “pairing” means

The Synap Cloud Control Plane can link two active pods you own into a pair: one primary and one replica.

- A link joins exactly two pods; the product does not offer a mesh.
- The Control Plane issues credentials and calls each pod's sync setup, so both sides agree on who pushes, who pulls
  and which secrets they share.
- Any other topology needs explicit configuration on the pods themselves.

## What gets replicated

| Area | Behaviour |
|------|-----------|
| Event log | Completed events are replicated continuously (fast path) and on a schedule (catch-up). |
| Workspace scope | A peer can be limited to specific workspaces. |
| Large files | Binary payloads follow a separate scheduled sync; their metadata travels in the event stream. |

Both metadata and payloads are replicated, and the replica applies them idempotently, so a duplicate delivery is
harmless.

## Trust and boundaries

- Replication runs between pods you configure (on Synap Cloud, pods in your account that you link). Peers
  authenticate with shared secrets and scoped roles; nothing is merged across tenants.
- Conflicts are resolved last-write-wins when events are materialized, and each conflict is recorded for audit.
- Other merge rules (per-entity rules, CRDTs for selected types) do not exist.

## What it does not do

- Link three or more pods into one cluster from the Cloud UI.
- Offer a screen to resolve conflicts field by field.
- Guarantee a replication latency. The fast path plus the scheduled catch-up lets a short outage recover without a
  manual dump.
